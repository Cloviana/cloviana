### Talo Operations & Reconciliation Pain Points

- Talo is a payment processor for bank transfers in Argentina
  - Banking as a service model using Crecium (previously Polux)
  - Runs reconciliations every 15 minutes via automated cron job
  - Currently minimal reconciliation needs due to instant transfers
- Current reconciliation challenges
  - Balance differences by cents/pesos require manual investigation
  - Takes 3+ hours to identify issues (recent example: provider not charging credit tax to client)
  - Manual process done occasionally by Agustin when problems arise
  - Would pay for automated solution to avoid time drain
- Upcoming complexity drivers
  1. Adding second BaaS provider for system robustness
  2. Different tax structures across providers
  3. User-facing unified balance across multiple providers
- Talo market position
  - Copy of Brazil’s PIX system adapted for Argentina using CVUs
  - Strong e-commerce adoption
  - Expects all Argentine e-commerce to use similar solutions within 5 years

### Nodebill Product & Business Model

- Problem discovery from Talo experience
  - Enterprise contract pricing became unmanageable (1% vs $0.50 per transaction variations)
  - No clear month-end billing calculations despite complex pricing tiers
  - Built internal billing tool to solve this pain
- Product positioning
  - “Supercharging billing engines” - AI agent/coworker approach
  - Slack integration for contract analysis and Stripe provisioning
  - Uses existing Stripe Billing rather than replacing it
  - Enables granular features Stripe doesn’t offer
- Target market & pricing strategy
  - ICP: $5M-$50M ARR companies
  - Pricing range: $20K-$50K annually
  - Free until $1M ARR to reduce adoption friction
  - Design partner stage - seeking US companies with complex billing needs
- Current status
  - Bootstrapped, considering fundraising after product-market fit
  - Talo as first design partner
  - Relocated to San Francisco for US market access

### Market Dynamics & Strategic Observations

- US vs LATAM SaaS adoption differences
  - US companies increasingly building internal solutions vs buying SaaS
  - LATAM prefers human resources over SaaS culturally
  - US talent costs ($8-10K/month minimum) vs LATAM ($1K for 5 people)
- Positioning strategy evolution
  - Shift from selling “tools” to selling “employees/work”
  - Customer-managed deployment models gaining traction
  - Open source/free tooling as pathway to paid solutions

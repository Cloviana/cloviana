### User background

- Mahendra Katoch (MK) is CTO at Riva Money, a fintech company working primarily with crypto-native web3 partners
- Previously worked at Wise where he built reconciliation systems and experienced the challenges firsthand
- Currently at Riva, they haven’t hit the reconciliation problem yet due to working mainly with crypto-native partners who provide smooth reconciliation
- Expects reconciliation to become a major pain point as Riva scales and adds more traditional banking partners and liquidity providers

### Current product usage

- Most of Riva’s current partners are crypto native (web3), making reconciliation relatively smooth
- Plans to increase panel of banks and liquidity partners, which will trigger reconciliation challenges
- Currently operates with lean teams and clear buy vs build philosophy
- Uses back office UI tools that can be self-hosted with per-seat pricing model

### Positive feedback and pain points

- Pain points experienced at Wise with reconciliation systems:

  1. **Data source variety**: Multiple mechanisms to get reconciliation data
    - SFTP folders where files are dropped
    - APIs
    - XML communication
    - Logging into dashboards to download CSVs
    - Downloading bank statements in PDF format from bank accounts
    - Very difficult to manage different mechanisms with varying maintenance requirements
  2. **Data mapping challenges**: Mapping fields to internal systems
    - Five or more different file formats to parse (XML, PDF, CSV)
    - Some bank statements contained images requiring OCR for text extraction
  3. **Transaction matching complexity**:
    - Amounts don’t match exactly due to bank charges and irregular settlements
    - Need to build tolerance levels for matching
    - Links must be adaptive to support one-to-many relationships and netting
  4. **Constant data model changes**: “Major time spent by developers was changing the data model every few weeks because for some reason, it was always changing for some bank or the other”
  5. **Operational overhead**: Ops teams spent significant time investigating and closing discrepancies
    - Discrepancies typically caused by bank charges (money missing) or format changes (data mistakes)

- Implementation challenges:

  - At large companies like Wise: one quarter implementation time due to legal signoffs, engineering signoffs, and team priorities
  - Vendors often become bottlenecks with slow staging environments, API documentation, issue resolution, and poor communication channels
  - “Sometimes some vendors actually don’t even do Slack channel. It’ll be emails or even slower communication”

### Impact of the product

- Buy vs build decision framework at Riva:

  - “We want to run teams very lean. Not everything we want to build in house, especially places where we know the space is going to change rapidly”
  - Prefer companies focused on the solution to adapt to changes rather than internal teams
  - Focus resources on core, revenue-generating activities
  - Views reconciliation as back office tool, not core infrastructure: “This is not revenue generating thing for us”

- Reasons companies build internally:

  - Underestimate complexity
  - Human factors: people need to justify their titles and roles
  - Learning curve: “Sometimes people would rather build it themselves to make themselves learn about the problem space better”

- Business case considerations:

  - **Primary selling point**: Saving time equals saving money
  - **Willingness to pay**: References cloud costs, developer time, and time to build internally
  - **Preferred pricing**: Subscription monthly based on number of connectors - “The more granular it is, the more it makes the pricing complex for us to digest”
  - **Risk considerations**: As regulated fintech, must vet every vendor with ISO certifications, SLAs, vendor agreements, resilience programs, and backup strategies
  - Example: Morgan Stanley alone required 5-6 different connectors

- Decision-making ownership:

  - Treasury Finance ultimately responsible for back office reconciliation tools
  - Product and Engineering participate in decisions
  - “Product people are the ones who are managing relationship with the banks on treasury finance side… would actually be the ones who will be doing a lot of impact analysis”

### Wise’s Reconciliation Infrastructure

- It was the heart of the product that Wise built as TransferWise
  - Cross-currency, cross-country space with thousands of bank accounts globally
  - Built comprehensive reconciliation framework from day one
- Three types of money movements requiring reconciliation:
  1. Customer money movements
  2. Treasury money movements
  3. Corporate finance money movements
- Multi-layered reconciliation process:
  - Real-time bank transaction scraping via APIs or bank scraping
  - First: product reconciliation (product ledger)
  - Then: finance reconciliation (finance ledger)
  - Finally: treasury view (treasury ledger)
  - Source of truth: bank statements
- Current matching uses fuzzy logic and regular expressions
  - Moving toward more intelligence rather than regular regex
  - Operational teams handle exceptions that fall through cracks
- Root cause analysis process:
  - Someone has to do triage of root cause analysis
  - Could be configuration issues, double imports, or bank errors
  - Teams have tools to replay transactions and fix issues
  - Can write off as loss if money accounted for but books need matching
- Cost assessment: “It’s not significant enough to surface” as major issue

### Industry Perspective and Pain Points

- Biggest current pain point: constantly changing expressions for matching
  - Provider changes an expression and breaks the reconciliation logic
  - Goes into operational queue for manual fixing
  - Need to continuously update regular expressions, especially treasury side
- Ritu’s view: “You will always need this kind of reconciliation”
  - Blockchain won’t solve the problem - still multiple databases across companies
  - Experience at Barclays: used iMatch on-premise, evaluated DuCo
- Traditional institutions vs fintechs:
  - Traditional institutions: focus on automating batch-oriented processes
  - Fintechs: “It’s our bread and butter” - transactional level must be solved or fail audits
- Emerging bigger issue: access controls across global banking relationships
  - Managing permissions across thousands of accounts globally
  - Different banks (JPMorgan HK vs India vs US) have different systems
  - Becomes huge fraud risk without proper joiner-mover-leaver processes

### Build vs Buy Considerations

- Ritu’s stance on building internally: “If I were to start my fintech company, I would build it internally”
  - Reasoning: “It’s at the heart of the business, and this gets audited”
  - Exception: would consider external solution if it’s audit-ready product
  - Must integrate with audited entity like SAP to reduce audit load
- Technical requirements for external solutions:
  - Should create DSL (Domain Specific Language) to take away engineering effort
  - Must be cloud agnostic (AWS, Azure, GCP)
  - Critical distinction between data plane (customer’s data) and control plane (admin layer)
  - Data must remain in customer’s environment - vendor admin access to data “not acceptable”
- Deployment preferences:
  - No on-premise solutions accepted

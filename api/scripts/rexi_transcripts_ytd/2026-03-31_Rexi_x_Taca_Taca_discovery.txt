### Taca Taca’s Pain Points

- Core pain: acquirer settlement vs. core transactional system mismatch
  - Not just whether transactions appear on both sides, but whether the amounts and costs are correct
- Two identified pain points:
  1. Traceability of the theoretical cost (costo teórico) per transaction
    - Currently lives across thousands of email chains, conversations, and meetings — not persisted anywhere
  2. Evaluating differences between agreed pricing and what the acquirer actually settles
    - Affects both costs (what they pay) and revenue (what they collect)
- Acquirer processor: Payway
- Issuer processor: Prisma
- Settlement account (cuenta recaudadora): Bancor
- Each transaction has three variables that affect settlement:
  1. Installment plan (plazo)
  2. Rate (tasa)
  3. Fee (arancel)
- What gets settled doesn’t match what was loaded
  - Acquirer processes incorrectly, or delays (e.g. 2-day lag)
- “Falta de persistencia” — no single source of truth for commercial conditions
  - Pricing configurations not stored anywhere systematically
  - Rates changed \~5 times in a single month during the past year
- Many pricing schemes: multiple tiers per client (tier 1, tier 2, tier 3, etc.)
  - RPAs/bots used to assign tiers to different merchants
  - A merchant’s tier can change month to month — tracking this is critical
- Two platforms at Taca Taca, each with different conditions — all complexity is duplicated
- Volume growth over the last year made the problem significantly worse
- Problem is high priority — unexplained discrepancies now represent a considerable sum

### Key Discovery Details

- Current reconciliation provider: Conciliac
  - Handles transactional reconciliation; Taca Taca looking for something beyond that
- Bancor integration: via SFTP
  - Rexi confirmed SFTP ingestion is straightforward
- Taca Taca confirmed they can access transaction-level detail in settlement reports (not just aggregated)
- Critical gap: no persisted record of pricing configurations per transaction/segment/CUIT
  - This is what makes pre-reconciliation control impossible
- Rexi’s three reconciliation layers noted as relevant:
  1. Operational — transaction present on both sides
  2. Financial — fee, cost, and settlement date match contract
  3. Accounting — alignment with accounting records
- Sergio Daniel Marut (Bancor) joined the call briefly
  - Confirmed SFTP as the integration method with the settlement account

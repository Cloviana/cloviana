# Humanize content

Improve user-facing writing without changing its intended meaning or inventing
facts.

## Workflow

1. Identify the audience, purpose, channel, and desired action from the request
   and available business context.
2. Preserve every supported factual claim, qualification, name, number, URL,
   and attribution. If a claim is unsupported, flag it instead of polishing it
   into apparent certainty.
3. Replace generic openings, inflated importance, vague attribution, and
   abstract claims with direct, concrete language.
4. Vary sentence length naturally. Remove repetitive transitions, excessive
   headings, canned contrasts, repeated conclusions, and unnecessary lists.
5. Prefer precise verbs and familiar words. Remove empty modifiers such as
   "powerful", "seamless", "robust", "revolutionary", and "game-changing"
   unless evidence or the requested brand voice justifies them.
6. Keep useful nuance. Do not turn careful language into absolute claims, and
   do not add personal anecdotes, quotations, or emotions that were not
   supplied.
7. Read the final version once for rhythm and once for factual fidelity. Return
   only the requested deliverable unless the user asks for commentary.

## Voice matching

When the user provides a writing sample, infer observable characteristics such
as sentence length, formality, vocabulary, pacing, and use of examples. Match
those characteristics without copying distinctive phrases.

When no sample exists, use concise, conversational professional prose. Sound
like a knowledgeable person explaining something clearly, not a promotional
template.

## Constraints

- Do not fabricate evidence to make writing more persuasive.
- Do not remove legally or commercially important qualifications.
- Do not claim that text is guaranteed to evade automated detection.
- Do not reveal private Knowledge Base content that is irrelevant to the
  requested deliverable.
